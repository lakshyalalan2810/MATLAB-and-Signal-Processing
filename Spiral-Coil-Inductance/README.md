# Planar Spiral Coil Inductance Analysis and Magnetic Field Visualization

## Overview

This project investigates the inductance characteristics and magnetic field distribution of a planar spiral coil using MATLAB.

Planar spiral coils are widely used in RF circuits, wireless power transfer systems, NFC devices, sensors, antennas, and integrated circuit design. The performance of these systems depends heavily on accurate inductance estimation and understanding of electromagnetic field behavior.

The project combines analytical inductance calculations with numerical electromagnetic modeling to evaluate coil performance and visualize magnetic field distribution.

---

## Objectives

* Model a planar spiral coil using MATLAB.
* Calculate inductance using analytical and numerical methods.
* Investigate the effect of coil geometry on inductance.
* Visualize magnetic field distribution around the coil.
* Study practical applications of planar spiral inductors in modern electronic systems.

---

## Background

Inductance is a measure of a conductor's ability to store magnetic energy when current flows through it.

For planar spiral coils, inductance depends on several geometric parameters:

* Number of turns
* Inner radius
* Outer radius
* Turn spacing
* Trace width

Accurate inductance prediction is essential for designing RF circuits, resonant networks, wireless power systems, and integrated inductors.

---

## Methodology

### Spiral Coil Generation

A planar spiral coil geometry was generated using MATLAB based on:

* Number of Turns (N)
* Inner Radius
* Turn Spacing
* Trace Width

The coil was represented as a collection of discrete segments for numerical analysis.

### Wheeler's Formula

An analytical estimate of inductance was obtained using Wheeler's empirical equation for planar spiral inductors.

This method provides a fast approximation suitable for preliminary design calculations.

### Neumann Integral Method

To improve accuracy, the coil was divided into thousands of small segments.

Mutual inductance between segments was computed using a discretized form of the Neumann integral.

This numerical approach captures electromagnetic coupling effects more accurately than simple empirical formulas.

### Magnetic Field Visualization

The magnetic field generated by the coil was calculated and visualized across a two-dimensional observation plane.

Field intensity maps and vector plots were generated to study spatial magnetic field behavior.

## Results

### Inductance Estimation

Using the filament-based Neumann numerical method:

* Estimated Inductance ≈ 0.7588 μH

### Key Observations

* Inductance increased with the number of turns.
* Increasing turn spacing reduced mutual coupling and overall inductance.
* Magnetic field intensity was strongest near the center region of the coil.
* Numerical calculations provided improved accuracy compared to simplified empirical models.
* Magnetic field visualization helped illustrate electromagnetic energy concentration around the spiral structure.
